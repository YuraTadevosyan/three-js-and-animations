This website is alive.

Not animated — alive. One heartbeat drives everything that moves here. The sky breathes, the interface follows your cursor, the garden further down keeps growing while this tab is closed, the weather drifts on its own, and the colour of every element on screen is a function of the actual time where you are sitting.

See what's alive How it's built

Nothing here is on a timeline. Leave it alone and it gets drowsy, then sleeps. Come back tomorrow and the plants will have grown without you.

01 — Respiration

It breathes

A single oscillator runs the whole page. The horizon in the sky shader lifts on the inhale, cards ease forward, buttons swell by half a percent, and the headline's letterforms soften — all from the same number.

The curve is asymmetric on purpose. A real inhale takes about forty percent of the cycle and the exhale takes the rest, with a brief hold at the top. Make it a clean sine wave and it stops reading as breathing and starts reading as a pulsing div.

The rate is not fixed either. Move around and it climbs toward sixteen breaths a minute; sit still and it settles to seven; let it fall asleep and it drops to five.

02 — Attention

It watches you

Cursor position is the page's only sense organ, so a lot is made of it. Every eye on this page shares one pointer stream and one set of springs; what varies is where each element sits, which is enough to make six faces look like six creatures rather than six copies.

The page also measures how much you are moving, not just where. That figure drives the breath rate, the pupils, the strength of the glow around your cursor, and how long it takes to get drowsy.

  • Tracks

    Pupils aim at the cursor itself, not at a bounding box, so the gaze stays honest at any angle.

  • Dilates

    Pupils open in the dark and when you move fast, and contract in full daylight.

  • Blinks

    On its own schedule, in clusters, and at anything you click.

  • Searches

    Leave the window and the eyes saccade around looking for where you went.

  • Sags

    Lids drop as the page gets drowsy, and close when it falls asleep.

  • Leans

    Buttons within reach pull toward the cursor before you arrive at them.

Both pull toward your cursor from about 170 pixels away.

03 — Growth

It grows

Each plant is one integer. Species, branching angles, curl, leaf spacing, flower size, colour shift and temperament are all re-derived from that seed, so the entire garden persists in a few hundred bytes of local storage.

Growth is real time, not a loop. Plants photosynthesise faster in daylight, drink when it rains, wilt in drought, and slow down but never stop at night. Around fifteen minutes of good conditions takes a seedling to maturity.

Then they flower, go to seed, drop one child each, and fade back into the soil — which raises the fertility for whatever grows next. Close the tab and it keeps going at a reduced rate. Come back in a week and you will find grandchildren.

04 — Weather & sun

The sky moves on

Weather walks a Markov chain. Every ninety seconds to three and a half minutes the current system rolls for a successor, weighted so it can only move to something adjacent — clear never jumps straight to storm, it has to get there through fair, cloudy and rain. That constraint is the whole reason the sky feels continuous instead of shuffled.

Today's real date picks the season, which re-weights the table: snow is common in winter and impossible in summer, storms peak in July, mist belongs to autumn. Aurora only rolls after dark.

The sun runs on the standard sunrise equation against the real calendar day, so the night is genuinely long in December and short in June. Everything you see is a crossfade — no system ever cuts.

05 — Circadian colour

It keeps your hours

There is no dark mode here, and no dark: variant anywhere in the markup. Seven palettes are pinned around the clock — deep night, first light, sunrise, midday, golden hour, dusk — and the current time is interpolated between the two it falls between, hue by hue, on the short way round the wheel.

The result is written straight onto :root as the same HSL custom properties Tailwind compiles against. So bg-primary is a different green at noon than it is at nine in the evening, and every component follows without knowing anything about it.

Writing custom properties on the root element invalidates style for the entire document, so this runs at eight hertz rather than sixty, and skips any value that has not changed. The sun does not move fast enough for anyone to notice the difference.

06 — Anatomy

One heartbeat

The rule that makes this hang together: there is exactly one requestAnimationFrame loop on the page, and every system subscribes to it. CSS animations and per-component loops each run on their own timeline and drift apart within seconds — which is precisely why most animated pages read as a pile of unrelated widgets rather than one thing.

Motion is used only for transitions that have a beginning and an end: scroll reveals, the notice that appears when you come back, the caption under the garden. Continuous motion belongs to the heartbeat. One writer per property, always.

Three substrates

A WebGL sky in PixiJS, a garden in SVG, and the interface in plain DOM — all reading the same state object on the same frame.

Light-DOM web components

Lit elements render into themselves rather than a shadow root, so one Tailwind sheet and one set of CSS variables reach every organ.

Zero binary assets

Clouds, rain, snow and sun flares are all drawn into offscreen canvases at boot. Nothing is fetched and nothing pops in.

Organs sleep off-screen

Each element only ticks while it intersects the viewport, so an idle tab costs one shader draw instead of nine live systems.

It degrades quietly

No WebGL leaves a correct CSS gradient. No local storage starts a fresh garden. No JavaScript at all still leaves a readable page.

It respects the setting

Under prefers-reduced-motion the rhythm keeps running so timings stay correct, but the amplitude collapses to nothing.

Open the console and poke at window.organism. Everything on this page is one readable state object.
